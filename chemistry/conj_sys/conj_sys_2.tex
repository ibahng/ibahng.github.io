    \begin{core}{Secondary Orbital Overlap}

      Under certain circumstances, orbitals not directly involved in new bond formation between the diene and dienophile can undergo secondary overlap, thereby lowering the overall E$_\text{a}$. Consider the following Diels-Alder interaction between two cyclopentenes.

      \myfig{0.75}{conj_sys_figures/secondary_overlap}{Secondary orbital overlap during an endo Diels-Alder reaction}{fig:ph53}
      \vspace{-0.4cm}

      It is important to note that although favorable mixing of secondary orbital overlaps within the transition state helps stabilize that transition state, steric factors nonetheless disfavor the endo approach. Consequently, ambiguous diene-dienophile pairs typically require experimental investigation to determine which effect predominates in a given case.

    \end{core}

    \begin{core}{Electronic Effects}

      Diels-Alder reactions proceed more rapidly when the energies of the interacting orbitals are closer to one another. In other words, the smaller the HOMO-LUMO gap between the reacting molecules, the faster the reaction rate. This condition is typically achieved through increased conjugation.

      However, while increased conjugation typically narrows the HOMO-LUMO gap and accelerates the reaction, it also alters the orbital's wave topology (\textit{i.e., the arrangement of its nodes}). If the orbital possessing the ideal energy gap happens to place a node on an essential bonding atom, that orbital becomes unusable for the reaction, forcing the system to proceed via a higher-energy, slower pathway instead. Symmetry and orbital overlap must therefore always be satisfied before energy gap considerations become relevant.

    \end{core}
